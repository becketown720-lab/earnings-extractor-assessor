Cibus Reports Third Quarter Financial Results and Provides Business Update

Rice herbicide-tolerance traits performed well in Latin American field trials and are now being tested by customer Interoc in its own varieties

Initiated the manufacturing process for initial two biofragrance products; biofragrance program represents an estimated $100 million peak annual revenue opportunity with eight products currently identified for potential commercialization

Year-to-date cash used in operating activities down approximately 19% year-over-year; continues to anticipate exiting 2026 at approximately $35 million annualized net cash usage run rate

SAN DIEGO, November 12, 2026 – Cibus, Inc. (Nasdaq: CBUS) (the "Company"), a technology company that uses biology to produce sustainable ingredients and helps farmers grow more food with fewer inputs, today announced its financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, and provided a business update. Management will host a conference call and webcast today at 4:30 p.m. ET.

Management Commentary

Craig Wichner, Chief Executive Officer of Cibus, commented, "Three months ago I described three ways our platform creates value: platform programs that generate revenue today, royalties as our traits reach acres, and partnerships that deepen over time. Each of these moved closer this quarter. We received requests from our customer for larger quantities of two biofragrance products to advance their product development. Our Rice traits performed well in Latin American field trials and are now being tested where it counts, in our customer Interoc's own varieties in its own fields. Further, a second partner-funded crop program, Mustard in India, began work in the quarter."

Third Quarter and Recent Business Update

The following summarizes Cibus' commercial, regulatory, and corporate progress during the third quarter and through the date of this release.

Priority Pipeline Traits and Programs

•Weed Management (HT1 and HT3) in Rice
◦The traits are now in field testing in customer Interoc's own Rice varieties, planted in Ecuador in late September 2026, with results expected in the first quarter of 2027.
◦Both traits performed well in 2026 Latin American field trials in test varieties, planted in April with results received in August 2026. These trials also produce the data customers need to expand herbicide labels to Rice carrying Cibus' traits.

◦Cibus continues to advance toward a definitive commercialization agreement with Interoc. Such an agreement would give Interoc the right to sell commercializable seed carrying Cibus traits in its markets and would pay Cibus a royalty on the acres planted with seed carrying its traits with initial commercial launch as early as 2028.
◦Cibus has seven Rice seed-company customers across Latin America and the United States. Latin America is the primary driver of near-term royalty revenue. Latin America represents the bulk of the roughly $200 million annual addressable royalty opportunity Cibus has estimated for its Rice trait portfolio across an estimated 5-7 million peak addressable acres.
◦Cibus is preparing a trait regulatory dossier for authorization to import gene-edited Rice trait material into Brazil, and is continuing discussions with participants in the Indian Rice market.
◦In the United States, the launch is paired with Albaugh's herbicide-registration timeline, and current planning targets a 2029 or 2030 U.S. launch.

•Sustainable Ingredients Program (Partially partner-funded)
◦Generating revenue under the Company's collaboration agreements and continuing a commercial ramp-up phase with its consumer-products partner.
◦Cibus' initial biofragrances are produced in proprietary yeast and extracted after fermentation, rather than pumped from petroleum or harvested at scale from plants.
◦When fully commercialized and assuming an eight-ingredient portfolio, the Company believes its biofragrance business could represent up to approximately $100 million in peak annual revenue. Cibus is now operating under, and these potential peak annual revenues reflect, a direct-sales model rather than the previously-assumed royalty model.
◦The partner has confirmed that both of the Company's first two biofragrance ingredients are approved for use in its products.
◦Initiated manufacturing process for the Company's anticipated biofragrance production runs.
◦Cibus presented additional biofragrances to its partner and subsequently, larger quantities of two of these additional biofragrances have been requested to advance their product development.
◦The Company's partner continues to evaluate the Company's additional fragrance ingredients, which are in development using the Company's proprietary technology and process.
◦Continuing to advance a sustainable ingredients program in Soybean, partially funded by a consumer packaged goods partner.

Corporate and Industry Progress

•Capital discipline and operational efficiency: Continue to anticipate exiting 2026 at an annual net cash usage run-rate of approximately $35 million. During 2026 the Company implemented several initiatives to structurally reduce its cost base, including resource reallocations as well as workforce reductions, consolidating its laboratory activities at its primary Nancy Ridge facility in San Diego, and a review of all corporate and back-office functions that lowered support-function costs, reduced spending on consultants and other external vendors, and tightened controls over travel and other discretionary expenditures. The Company also implemented several initiatives aimed at efficiency and productivity gains, including simplifying its legal-entity structure, improving integration of its financial systems, and increased use of technology and AI-enabled tools where appropriate.
•Cash used in operating activities was $9.3 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026. Net cash used in operating activities was $30.2 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared to $37.2 million in the year-ago period, an improvement of approximately $7.0 million, or approximately 19%.
•Cost structure and capital allocation review: As previously discussed, the Company conducted a review of its cost structure and capital allocation. That review reinforced the Company's focus on the programs with the clearest near-term commercial opportunities, Sustainable Ingredients and Rice, while preserving targeted investment in programs that management believes could create significant longer-term value, including nutrient-use efficiency and disease resistance. The Company intends to limit incremental growth investments to its highest-priority opportunities and to manage those investments within its cash usage objectives.
•Partner- and government-funded programs: In the third quarter of 2026 the Company began invoicing under a new partner-funded research program for Indian Mustard, recognized in revenue. Non-operating income for the quarter included grant funding for the Light Leaf Spot research program in the United Kingdom and a grant supporting its Soybean work.

Opportunity Pipeline Traits and Programs (Available for Partnership)

Cibus has demonstrated regeneration of whole plants from single cells in nine crops on its platform: Rice, Canola/Winter Oilseed Rape, Wheat, Flax, Peanut, Potato, Sugar Beet, Cassava, and Alfalfa; additional crops such as Soybean and Indian Mustard are in development. Cibus is actively developing traits in five crops: Rice, Canola/Winter Oilseed Rape, Soybean, Alfalfa, and Indian Mustard. Because Cibus' operational platforms are already built, a program in any of these crops can begin with trait work in those platforms rather than with years of platform development. Cibus' work helps position partners to establish market leadership for their crops.

•India (Mustard): In September 2026, Cibus announced an agreement with Crystal Crop Protection Limited to develop gene-edited traits in Mustard (Brassica juncea), which

Crystal Crop will evaluate and potentially commercialize in India. India grows more Mustard than any other country, Mustard is almost a third of its domestic oilseed production, and India imports more than half of the edible oil it consumes; Indian Mustard yields run roughly a third below those of the leading producing countries. India's National Mission on Edible Oils, launched in 2024, targets nearly doubling the country's oilseed harvest by 2030-31 and names genome editing among the tools to get there. In the third quarter of 2026 Cibus began work toward regenerating whole plants from single cells of Crystal Crop's own Mustard varieties, the step every new crop goes through to establish a crop platform using Cibus' Direct BreedingSM system.
•Nutrient-Use Efficiency (Root Microbe Symbiosis): The trait targets how a plant's roots take up fertilizer, the whole package rather than nitrogen alone, with potential application across Rice, Wheat, and Canola. Cibus has prepared multiple edited Canola seed lines, with their unedited parent, for transfer to the John Innes Centre for evaluation. The transfer, previously expected in the third quarter of 2026, is now expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. The Centre will evaluate the trait in spring oilseed rape under a sponsored research agreement, and Cibus is beginning the same edit in Rice.
•Canola: Light Leaf Spot is one of the most damaging diseases of the United Kingdom's oilseed rape crop, costing growers an estimated £300 million in 2022 alone (a particularly severe outbreak year), and the fungicides that controlled it are losing their efficacy. A consortium of farmers, breeders and research institutes, funded in part through DEFRA's Farming Innovation Programme, chose Cibus to make the resistance edit in elite winter oilseed rape, and that work is advancing on schedule. Pod shatter reduction prevents seed pods from splitting open and dropping their seed before harvest. Following three years of UK field trials in customer germplasm, Cibus filed a notification with DEFRA during the third quarter of 2026 for the release of its precision bred pod-shatter-reduction plants and received approval for trial planting in England under the Precision Bred Organisms framework. Cibus' second-generation Canola herbicide tolerance trait, which gives growers another option against hard-to-control weeds in Canola, confirmed tolerance to its target herbicide in 2026 field trials at three locations, although drought limited yield data to one location.
•Soybean: Continuing to build toward an expected operational platform, which would also support the Company's sustainable ingredients program. During the quarter, Cibus validated editing reagents for that program, the step before attempting additional Soybean plant editing.
•Alfalfa: The Company's improved-quality Alfalfa trait is licensed to a third party for commercialization in the United States.

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results

•Cash position: Cash and cash equivalents as of September 30, 2026, was $10.7 million. Taking into account the impact of implemented cost saving initiatives, and without giving effect to potential financing transactions that Cibus may pursue from time-to-time, Cibus expects that existing cash and cash equivalents is sufficient to fund planned operating expenses and capital expenditure requirements into early in the first quarter of 2027, reflecting current cash usage expectations.

•Research and development (R&D) Expense: R&D expense was $7.9 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $10.8 million in the year-ago period. The decrease of $2.9 million is primarily due to a $1.5 million decrease in personnel-

related expenses primarily resulting from lower headcount, a $0.9 million decrease in facilities-related expenses primarily resulting from cost reduction initiatives, including the closure of the Oberlin facility and wind-down of operations at the Roseville, Minnesota facility, and a $0.4 million decrease in product development and lab supply costs primarily resulting from a streamlined focus and prioritization of development programs and lower headcount.

•Selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) expense: SG&A expense was $5.4 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $5.3 million in the year-ago period. The increase of $0.1 million is primarily due to a $0.1 million increase in facilities-related expenses primarily resulting from relocating equipment from the Roseville, Minnesota facility and a $0.1 million increase in professional fees, which is primarily the result of a $0.4 million increase from a litigation liability reduction in the prior-year period, partially offset by a $0.3 million decrease from cost reduction initiatives. These increases are partially offset by a $0.2 million decrease in personnel-related expenses primarily resulting from lower headcount.

•Royalty liability interest expense - related parties: Royalty liability interest expense - related parties (non-cash) was $9.8 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $9.0 million in the year-ago period. The increase of $0.8 million is due to the recognition of interest expense on the accumulating Royalty Liability balance.

•Non-operating income, net: Non-operating income, net was $0.4 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to a nominal amount in the year-ago period. The increase of $0.4 million is primarily driven by grant income recognized for research work performed by Cibus.

•Net loss: Net loss was $21.9 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $24.3 million in the year-ago period.

•Net loss per share of Class A common stock: Net loss per share of Class A common stock was $0.29 for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to net loss per share of Class A common stock of $0.44 in the year-ago period. The decrease of $0.15 in net loss per share of Class A common stock is primarily driven by the $2.4 million decrease in net loss drivers described above and a year-over-year increase in weighted average shares outstanding.

How Cibus does it

Cibus starts with a seed company's best existing varieties and makes precise improvements, often several at once, to a single cell from those varieties. It then grows that cell into a full plant carrying the new traits. The seed company keeps the variety it spent years perfecting; Cibus adds the improvements and licenses them back. Cibus' changes are precise edits to the plant's own genes, with no foreign DNA added, which is what separates them from transgenic GMOs. Cibus traits like disease resistance, and pod shatter reduction in Canola and Oilseed Rape, are generally expected to qualify for conventional-breeding treatment in key jurisdictions.

About Cibus

Cibus (Nasdaq: CBUS) is a technology company that helps farmers grow more food with fewer inputs. Using its proprietary platform, Cibus improves a seed company's best crop varieties by making precise changes to the plant's own genes, with no foreign DNA added, then licenses those improvements back to the customer in exchange for royalties. Cibus is not a seed company. Cibus calls its platform Direct BreedingSM. It develops crop traits at a fraction of the time and cost of conventional breeding, with a focus on higher yields, better quality, and reduced chemical use. For more information, visit www.Cibus.com.
