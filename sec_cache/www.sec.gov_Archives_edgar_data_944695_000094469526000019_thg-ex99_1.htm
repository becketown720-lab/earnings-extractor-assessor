The Hanover Reports Excellent Third Quarter Net Income and

Operating Income of $4.86 and $5.15 per Diluted Share, Respectively;

Third Quarter Net and Operating Return on Equity of 19.4% and 18.7%, Respectively

 

Third Quarter Highlights

•
Combined ratio of 92.3%; combined ratio, excluding catastrophes(1), of 85.5%
•
Catastrophe losses of $109.7 million, or 6.8 points of the combined ratio
•
Net premiums written increase of 3.1%*
•
Renewal price increases(2) of 8.7% in Personal Lines, 7.4% in Core Commercial and 4.3% in Specialty
•
Rate increases(2) of 6.1% in Core Commercial, 4.9% in Personal Lines and 1.9% in Specialty
•
Loss and loss adjustment expense (LAE) ratio of 61.4%, 1.6 points higher than the prior-year quarter, driven by higher catastrophe losses
•
Current accident year loss and LAE ratio, excluding catastrophes(3), of 55.7%, 1.9 points below the prior-year quarter
•
Net investment income of $131.9 million, up 12.7% from the prior-year quarter
•
Book value per share of $99.69, down 5.4% from June 30, 2026; excluding net unrealized depreciation on fixed maturity investments, net of tax(4), book value per share increased 2.7%

 

WORCESTER, Mass., October 28, 2026 - The Hanover Insurance Group, Inc. (NYSE: THG) today reported net income of $172.6 million, or $4.86 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $178.7 million, or $4.90 per diluted share, in the prior-year quarter. Operating income(5) was $183.1 million, or $5.15 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $185.6 million, or $5.09 per diluted share, in the prior-year quarter. The company reported net and operating return on equity(6) of 19.4% and 18.7%, respectively, in the third quarter of 2026, and 20.6% and 19.6% in the first nine months of 2026, respectively.

 

“In the third quarter, we continued to translate strategic clarity, disciplined execution and the breadth of our diversified franchise into exceptional performance,” said John C. Roche, president and chief executive officer at The Hanover. “With operating return on equity of 18.7% and record third quarter operating earnings per share of $5.15, our results reflect excellent underwriting execution and the benefits of our past portfolio actions. Our business mix continues to support strong performance and stable renewal pricing. A differentiated Specialty business, a preferred Personal Lines franchise, and a leading position with the top-performing agents in Core Commercial continue to provide avenues for profitable growth across market conditions, while we remain patient where conditions warrant greater selectivity,” added Roche. “At the same time, we continue to invest in technology, AI and other capabilities that are delivering meaningful business impact and strengthening our future position. We are confident in our outlook, and our goal of long-term profitability and value creation will continue to guide every underwriting and growth decision we make.”

“We are very pleased with our third quarter performance highlighted by nearly two points of improvement in our current accident year ex-catastrophe loss ratio, favorable reserve development, continued growth in net investment income, and the strength of our capital position,” said Jeffrey M. Farber, executive vice president and chief financial officer at The Hanover. “Net investment income increased 12.7%, driven by higher earned yields, strong cash flows and increased partnership income. The consistent capital generation of our business enabled us to repurchase approximately $86 million of stock during the quarter, totaling $263 million year-to-date through October 26th. As we discussed at our September Investor Day, profitability, earnings growth and capital allocation priorities continue to guide our decisions. With substantial financial flexibility, we remain well-positioned to invest in the business, return capital to shareholders and capture attractive opportunities as they emerge.”

Third Quarter Operating Highlights

 

Core Commercial

Core Commercial operating income before income taxes was $55.5 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $65.1 million in the third quarter of 2025. The Core Commercial combined ratio was 100.5%, compared to 97.3% in the prior-year quarter. Catastrophe losses in the third quarter of 2026 were $45.3 million, or 7.8 points of the combined ratio. This compared to catastrophe losses of $17.8 million, or 3.2 points, in the prior-year quarter.

 

Third quarter 2026 results included net favorable prior-year reserve development, excluding catastrophes, of $3.0 million, or 0.5 points, compared to $1.2 million, or 0.2 points, in the third quarter of 2025.

 

Core Commercial current accident year combined ratio, excluding catastrophes, decreased 1.1 points, to 93.2% in the third quarter of 2026, compared to 94.3% in the prior-year quarter. The current accident year loss and LAE ratio, excluding catastrophes, was 60.5%, approximating the prior-year quarter, as the company continues to prudently set loss ratio selections in liability coverages.

 

The expense ratio decreased by 1.0 point, to 32.7%, in the third quarter of 2026, compared to the prior-year quarter, reflecting fixed cost leverage and efficiency gains.

 

Net premiums written were $648.9 million in the third quarter of 2026, up 4.6% from the prior-year quarter, reflecting growth of 2.8% in small commercial and 6.7% in middle market. Core Commercial renewal price increases averaged 7.4%, including average rate increases of 6.1%.

Specialty

Specialty operating income before income taxes was $88.1 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $78.2 million in the third quarter of 2025. The Specialty combined ratio was 84.0%, compared to 84.9% in the prior-year quarter. Catastrophe losses in the third quarter of 2026 were $12.4 million, or 3.4 points of the combined ratio. This compared to catastrophe losses of $6.0 million, or 1.7 points, in the prior-year quarter.

 

Third quarter 2026 results included net favorable prior-year reserve development, excluding catastrophes, of $14.8 million, or 4.0 points, with widespread favorability. Net favorable prior-year reserve development, excluding catastrophes, was $10.0 million, or 2.8 points, in the third quarter of 2025.

 

Specialty current accident year combined ratio, excluding catastrophes, decreased 1.4 points, to 84.6% in the third quarter of 2026, from 86.0% in the prior-year quarter. The current accident year loss and LAE ratio, excluding catastrophes, of 47.7% in the third quarter of 2026 decreased 1.1 points compared to the prior-year quarter and was favorable to the company's long-term expectations for the segment, driven by lower-than-expected property losses.

 

Net premiums written were $391.6 million in the third quarter of 2026, up 3.3% from the prior-year quarter. Specialty renewal price increases averaged 4.3%, including average rate increases of 1.9%.

Personal Lines

Personal Lines operating income before income taxes was $103.6 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $101.1 million in the third quarter of 2025. The Personal Lines combined ratio was 89.7%, compared to 89.2% in the prior-year quarter. Catastrophe losses in the third quarter of 2026 were $52.0 million, or 7.9 points of the combined ratio. This compared to catastrophe losses of $22.4 million, or 3.5 points of the combined ratio, in the prior-year quarter.

 

Third quarter 2026 results included net favorable prior-year reserve development, excluding catastrophes, of $0.7 million, or 0.1 points, compared to $0.9 million, or 0.1 points, in the third quarter of 2025.

 

Personal Lines current accident year combined ratio, excluding catastrophe losses, decreased 3.9 points, to 81.9%, in the third quarter of 2026, from 85.8% in the prior-year quarter. The current accident year loss and LAE ratio, excluding catastrophes, decreased 3.8 points from the prior-year quarter, to 56.0%, driven by the continued benefit of earned pricing outpacing loss trends, as well as benign property claims frequency.

 

Net premiums written were $752.0 million in the third quarter of 2026, up 1.7% compared to the prior-year quarter. The increase was primarily due to the impact of renewal price increases and higher new business. Personal Lines renewal price increases averaged 8.7%, including average rate increases of 4.9%. Policies in force (PIF) in the third quarter of 2026 were essentially flat compared to the second quarter of 2026.

Investments

Net investment income was $131.9 million in the third quarter of 2026, an increase of 12.7% from the prior-year quarter, primarily due to the continued investment of cashflows from operations, the impact of higher earned yields on the fixed maturity investment portfolio, and higher investment income from limited partnerships. Total pre-tax earned yield on the investment portfolio for the third quarter of 2026 was 4.60%, up from 4.31% in the prior-year quarter. The average pre-tax earned yield on fixed maturities was 4.58% for the third quarter of 2026, up from 4.33% in the prior-year quarter.

 

Net realized and unrealized investment losses recognized in earnings were $14.1 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $9.0 million in the third quarter of 2025.

 

The company held $11.2 billion in cash and invested assets at September 30, 2026. Fixed maturities and cash represented approximately 93% of the investment portfolio. Approximately 95% of the company’s fixed maturity portfolio is rated investment grade. As of September 30, 2026, net unrealized losses on the fixed maturity portfolio were $636.1 million before income taxes, compared to $259.5 million at June 30, 2026.

 

Shareholders’ Equity and Capital Actions

At September 30, 2026, book value per share was $99.69, down 5.4% from June 30, 2026, as strong earnings were more than offset by an increase in the unrealized loss position on the fixed maturity portfolio, share repurchases and the ordinary quarterly cash dividends. Book value per share, excluding net unrealized depreciation on fixed maturity investments, net of tax, was $114.26 at September 30, 2026, up 2.7% from June 30, 2026, after the impact of share repurchases and the ordinary quarterly cash dividends.

 

At September 30, 2026, operating insurance company’s statutory capital and surplus was $3.70 billion, compared to $3.54 billion at June 30, 2026.

 

The company repurchased approximately 386,000 shares of common stock in the third quarter of 2026, totaling approximately $86 million. Year-to-date through October 26th, the company has repurchased approximately 1,334,000 shares, totaling approximately $263 million. The company has approximately $546 million of remaining capacity under its existing share repurchase authorization.

Definition of Segments

Continuing operations include four reporting segments: Core Commercial, Specialty, Personal Lines and Other. The Core Commercial segment includes commercial multiple peril, commercial automobile, workers’ compensation and other core commercial lines coverages provided to small and mid-sized businesses. The Specialty segment includes four divisions of business: marine and industrial property, professional and executive lines (such as management and professional liability), E&S and alternative markets, and surety and other. E&S and alternative markets includes coverages such as excess and surplus lines, program business (providing commercial insurance to markets with specialized coverage or risk management need related to groups of similar businesses), and specialty general liability coverage. The Personal Lines segment markets automobile, homeowners and ancillary coverages to individuals and families. The Other segment primarily includes the operations of the holding company, and run-off direct asbestos and environmental business, run-off voluntary assumed property and casualty pools business, and run-off product liability business.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

As discussed on page 39 of the company’s Annual Report on Form 10-K for the year ended December 31, 2025, the company uses non-GAAP financial measures as important measures of its operating performance, including operating income, operating income before interest expense and income taxes, operating income per diluted share, and components of the combined ratio, both excluding and/or including catastrophe losses, prior-year reserve development and the expense ratio. Management believes these non-GAAP financial measures are important indications of the company’s operating performance. The definition of other non-GAAP financial measures and terms can be found in the 2025 Annual Report on pages 61-64.

 

10

 

 

Operating income and operating income per diluted share are non-GAAP measures. They are defined as net income excluding the after-tax impact of net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses), gains and/or losses on the repayment of debt, other non-operating items, and results from discontinued operations. Net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses), which include changes in the fair value of equity securities still held, are excluded for purposes of presenting operating income, as they are, to a certain extent, determined by interest rates, financial markets and the timing of sales. Operating income also excludes net gains and losses from disposals of businesses, gains and losses related to the repayment of debt, costs to acquire businesses, restructuring costs, the cumulative effect of accounting changes, and certain other items. Operating income is the sum of the segment income from: Core Commercial, Specialty, Personal Lines, and Other, after interest expense and income taxes. In reference to one of the company’s four reporting segments, “operating income” is the segment income before both interest expense and income taxes. The company also uses “operating income per diluted share” (which is after both interest expense and income taxes). Operating income per share is calculated by dividing operating income by the weighted average number of diluted shares of common stock. Operating loss per share is calculated by dividing operating loss by the weighted average number of basic shares of common stock due to antidilution. The company believes that metrics of operating income and operating income in relation to its four reporting segments provide investors with a valuable measure of the performance of the company’s continuing businesses because they highlight the portion of net income attributable to the core operations of the business. Income from continuing operations is the most directly comparable GAAP measure for operating income (and operating income before income taxes) and measures of operating income that exclude the effects of catastrophe losses and/or prior-year reserve development. These non-GAAP measures should not be misconstrued as substitutes for income from continuing operations or net income determined in accordance with GAAP. A reconciliation of operating income to income from continuing operations and net income for the relevant periods is included on page 9 of this news release and in the Financial Supplement.

 

Operating return on equity (ROE) is a non-GAAP measure. See end note (6) for a detailed explanation of how this measure is calculated. Operating ROE is based on non-GAAP operating income. In addition, the portion of shareholder equity attributed to unrealized appreciation (depreciation) on fixed maturity investments, net of tax, is excluded. The company believes this measure is helpful in that it provides insight to the capital used by, and results of, the continuing business exclusive of interest expense, income taxes, and other non-operating items. These measures should not be misconstrued as substitutes for GAAP ROE, which is based on net income and shareholders’ equity of the entire company and without adjustments.

 

Book value per share is total shareholders’ equity divided by the number of common shares outstanding. Book value per share excluding net unrealized appreciation (depreciation) on fixed maturity investments, net of tax, is a non-GAAP measure and is total shareholders’ equity excluding the after-tax effect of unrealized appreciation (depreciation) on fixed maturities and market risk divided by the number of common shares outstanding.

 

The company may provide measures of operating income and combined ratios that exclude the impact of catastrophe losses (which in all respects include prior accident year catastrophe loss development). A catastrophe is a severe loss, resulting from natural or manmade events including, but is not limited to, hurricanes, tornadoes and other windstorms, hail, flood, earthquakes, fires, drought, explosions, severe winter weather and other convective storms, riots, and terrorism. Due to the unique characteristics of each catastrophe loss, there is an inherent inability to reasonably estimate the timing or loss amount in advance. The company believes a separate discussion excluding the effects of catastrophe losses is meaningful to understand the underlying trends and variability of earnings, loss and combined ratio results, among others.

11

 

 

 

Prior accident year reserve development, which can either be favorable or unfavorable, represents changes in the company’s estimate of costs related to claims from prior years. Calendar year loss and loss adjustment expense (LAE) ratios determined in accordance with GAAP, excluding prior accident year reserve development, are sometimes referred to as “current accident year loss ratios.” The company believes a discussion of loss and combined ratios excluding prior accident year reserve development is helpful since it provides insight into both estimates of current accident year results and the accuracy of prior-year estimates.

 

The loss and combined ratios in accordance with GAAP are the most directly comparable GAAP measures for the loss and combined ratios calculated excluding the effects of catastrophe losses and/or prior-year reserve development. The presentation of loss and combined ratios calculated excluding the effects of catastrophe losses and/or prior-year reserve development should not be misconstrued as substitutes for the loss and/or combined ratios determined in accordance with GAAP.
